Tune Tournament

What I still make is Tune Tournament shorts, a music-rating series where two songs go head to head and viewers vote on which one sounds better. There's 66 videos now, and the thing I'm proudest of is that I don't edit them by hand. I built a generator for it.

It's an idea I had for a while to use code to create YouTube videos. Two raw audio files are inputted, and one finished vertical video comes out. Behind the scenes, it finds the structural sections of each song straight from the raw audio signal, picks the most distinct sounding segments, snaps to the downbeat, arranges the audio clips together, and normalizes the loudness of both songs to the same level. Then, a second stage composites the entire whole vertical video, rendering text with shadow, rendering a looping background, tinting the background from each song's cover art, and animating the elements with ease-in-out frame by frame.

Activity Wheel

React, TypeScript, Vite, IndexedDB, Supabase

I built a spin-the-wheel activity decider that learns what activities you actually enjoy. You spin it, it picks something for you to do, and you provide feedback (love it, accept, skip it, reject, hate it). Over time your reactions shift the weights so the activities the wheel picks match your preferences better, without you having to type in any numbers. Originally I built it just for my own use, but I decided to make it public to use with friends as well.

It works fully offline in your browser by default and never makes a network call unless you sign in with Google, which moves your data to a private, row-level-secured PostgreSQL database via Supabase.

Flow Focus

React, TypeScript, Zustand, IndexedDB

I got tired of all the task apps I tried, and I failed tracking things I needed to do by hand, so I built Flow Focus to only show you one task at a time, whatever is the single most important thing to do right now I mainly used it for myself when I had a ton of competing work to do with different priorities.

To pick what to show, it sorts every task by how much "slack time" each one has (how long you can safely put it off before it becomes impossible to finish on time), and subtracts the hours you'll be asleep from that math. I liked turning a vague idea like "just tell me what to do" into a real web app I actually could use and benefit from myself.
